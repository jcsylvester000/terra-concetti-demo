The Coral at Anilao is a diving and resort community of **68 homes on 2.3 hectares**, on a hillside **60 to 80 metres above the sea** looking out to Sombrero and Tingloy Islands. Before a single home was drawn, the design team asked a simple question: how do you build on a slope like this without fighting it?

The answer runs through every part of the plan. Here is the story of how The Coral is being designed, from the masterplan down to the rooms. You can see the homes, sizes and masterplan on [The Coral's project page](/destinations/the-coral-anilao).

## Architecture that breathes

The guiding idea is that the architecture should breathe with the landscape and the sea. Walls give way to wide openings, so the line between inside and outside blurs, and sea air can move freely through every room.

**Passive cooling** does much of the work. Deep overhangs, louvres and awnings shade the walls. Cross and stack ventilation, with operable windows, draw the breeze through the house. The aim is comfort with far less air-conditioning, which matters on a tropical coast and for the carbon footprint of every home.

## Materials that belong to the place

The material palette is warm and natural: **mass timber, cross-laminated timber (CLT) and glue-laminated timber (GLT)**, treated against termites and fire. Green roofs, green façades and living walls cool the buildings and soften them into the hillside.

Water is part of the design too. Rainwater is planned to be harvested for gardens and cooling ponds, and bioswales and a community garden planted with native species manage runoff before it reaches the shore.

## Four ways to live on the hillside

The 68 homes take four forms, each suited to a different part of the slope:

- **Single detached villas** (23 homes): private villas of one to three bedrooms, each on its own lot, with terraces facing the sea.
- **Townhouse villas** (3 homes): generous two- and three-bedroom villas for larger families.
- **Treehouse villas** (6 homes): raised among the trees, with the canopy at eye level and the sea beyond.
- **Condominium residences** (36 homes): studios to two-bedroom residences in two stepped clusters.

The condominium clusters follow a few simple rules. Each level offers a mix of home types. Premium residences take the corners, where views and light are best. And the stepped, interlocking massing gives most homes a terrace and a view, while keeping the buildings low against the slope.

## A walkable estate

Cars stay at the edge. **Remote parking** keeps the paths between homes quiet and pedestrian, so the estate feels like a village rather than a subdivision. Behind the scenes, a support-staff facility and centralised solid-waste management keep the estate running cleanly.

At the heart of it all is a **premium dive center**, because The Coral is planned for people who come to Anilao for the water. Full concierge, in-house property management and an optional rental pool are planned to make owning here easy, even from a distance.

## Designed with the reef in mind

The Coral's name is a reminder of what lies below the hillside. Keeping runoff, waste and light away from the shore protects the reefs that make Anilao one of the most celebrated dive coasts in the Philippines.
